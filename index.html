This is a free, self-directed course in writing fiction. It is built on a single stubborn premise: that storytelling is not a gift you are born with but a craft made of nameable, learnable, practiceable parts — and that you get good at it the same boring, reliable way you get good at anything, by drilling the fundamentals in order until they stop being hard.The model here is Drawabox, the drawing course that refuses to let you draw a face until you can draw a believable box. We start with the sentence.

How this works: the lessons below run in sequence, from the smallest unit of fiction (the sentence) up to the largest (a finished story). Each one explains a single idea, shows it working, and ends with an exercise you actually do. Reading about writing is not writing; the reps are the whole point.Keep everything you write. Lessons later in the course deliberately send you back to dig up things you made early on.

Features: this site has a dark mode by default (toggle it top-right), wide margins that hold sidenotes like the one to your right instead of footnotes you have to hunt for, hover-preview popups on the lesson links below, and absolutely no accounts, tracking, paywalls, or popups begging for your email. It is, proudly, a small handmade thing.

Why model a writing course on a drawing course?

Because drawing pedagogy solved a problem writing pedagogy mostly hasn't: it broke an intimidating creative act into a ladder of concrete, gradeable skills you can practice in isolation. Most writing advice skips straight to "find your voice." We're going to build the ladder first.
